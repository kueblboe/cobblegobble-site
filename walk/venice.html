Venice in one walk 8 sights, 40 minutes

A self-guided walking tour of the Venice Old Town: 8 sights on one 2.8 km loop, about 40 minutes on foot, back where you started.

No two days to spare? Here is the short version: the big sights in one loop. I would take longer. I always take longer.

The stops

8 sights in Venice, in walking order.

Each stop says how far the next one is and which streets take you there.

  1. 1. Basilica di San Marco

    the start

    It closes the east end of this walk, and I would not hurry the last stretch. The face of it is a wall of columns and coloured stone fetched in from elsewhere, a piece at a time, and almost none of it matches. Go in and look down rather than up. The floor is not flat.

    Next: Palazzo Ducale, 250 m, about 4 min, along Piazzetta San Marco, Piazza San Marco, Piazzetta dei Leoncini, Calle de la Canonica.

  2. 2. Palazzo Ducale

    about 4 minutes of walking from the start

    You can go under one whole side of it without paying anybody, which is my favourite sort of architecture. Stop at the seaward corner and look hard at the facing: pink and white slabs set in a diamond pattern that makes masonry look woven. Then find the capitals on the two outer corners and count what is carved into them.

    Next: Ponte di Rialto, 610 m, about 9 min, along Fondamenta de la Canonica, Calle Larga San Marco, Calle dei Spechieri, Calle de Mezo, Calle dei Stagneri.

  3. 3. Ponte di Rialto

    about 13 minutes of walking from the start

    It is not one path, it is three. Two run along the outer edges and a third climbs up the middle, between two rows of little shops that stand right on the arch. Take an outer one going over and the middle one coming back, and you have had the same bridge two ways.

    Next: Ca' Farsetti, 340 m, about 5 min, along Ponte di Rialto, Riva del Carbon.

  4. Lunch break

    Enoteca Al Volto, Calle Cavalli 4081, about 0 m off the route, open Mon–Sun 10:00–16:00, 18:00–22:00.

    A quiet lane, Calle Cavalli, three hundred metres from the Rialto bridge, and the regulars are mostly Venetians. More on what to eat in Venice.

  5. 4. Ca' Farsetti

    about 18 minutes of walking from the start

    From the lane you get its back and not much else. Go round to Riva del Carbon and look at it from the water side, where the arches are, because that is the side it was made to be seen from. Almost everything on that bank is like this. The good face points at a street I cannot walk.

    Next: Campo Santo Stefano, 660 m, about 10 min, along Calle Corner Piscopia, Campo Daniele Manin, Calle de la Cortesia, Calle de la Mandola, Campo Sant' Anzolo, Calle dei Frati, Campo Santo Stefano.

  6. 5. Campo Santo Stefano

    about 28 minutes of walking from the start

    This is the one open ground on the walk where nothing is pressing in on you: long, wide, trees, and children playing ball, which almost nowhere else here allows. Find the stone well-head out on the paving. Every campo had one, and the ground was laid to run rain into the cistern beneath it. A floor with two jobs, and I have never met a better one.

    Next: Teatro La Fenice, 340 m, about 5 min, along Calle del Spezier, Campiello dei Calegheri, Calle de la Fenice.

  7. 6. Teatro La Fenice

    about 33 minutes of walking from the start

    Finding it is half the job. It hides on a small campo off the shopping lanes, with no space in front of it to step back and admire anything, and the lane that gets you there is called after it. Look for the water door on the canal side instead: people still turn up at it by boat.

    Next: Museo Correr, 320 m, about 5 min, along Calle drio la Chiesa, Piscina de Frezzeria, Calle de Piscina de Frezzaria, Frezzaria.

  8. 7. Museo Correr

    about 38 minutes of walking from the start

    The door is at the far end of the big piazza, the end furthest from the church, and the stair behind it is grander than most palaces manage. Go for the windows rather than the cases. They run the whole length of the square and look straight down onto the paving you just crossed. This is the one address in this city where I can study a floor from a seated position.

    Next: Campanile di San Marco, 190 m, about 3 min, along Piazza San Marco.

  9. 8. Campanile di San Marco

    about 41 minutes of walking from the start

    The thing worth knowing before you get there is that it is not attached to anything. You can make a complete circle of its foot, which you cannot do with anything else on this piazza. At the base, facing the water, sits a small marble porch that has nothing to do with bells. There is also a lift, and I have no opinion about that whatsoever.

    Back to the start: Basilica di San Marco, 80 m, about 1 min, along Piazzetta San Marco.
